# Metformin for knee osteoarthritis: six meta-analyses in nine months, a fivefold spread in pain effect, and one trial published twice (2026-10-15)

MV2 of Munim, Inc., with AI agents (Claude) working in his name. Text CC BY 4.0. Research information, not medical advice.

## Summary

- **Six meta-analyses in nine months.** Between 2025-10-09 and 2026-07-22, six systematic reviews with meta-analyses of metformin for osteoarthritis were published online. Their pooled pain effects (standardized mean differences) run from -0.42 to -2.04, a nearly fivefold spread for the same drug and condition.
- **The one large, registered, double-blind trial is at the bottom of that range.** In Pan et al., JAMA 2025 (n = 107), the effect on pain was SMD 0.43 (95% CI 0.02-0.83), reported in the improvement direction. That is a VAS difference of -11.4 mm, below the trial's own 15 mm minimal clinically important difference. Pooled values above 1 must therefore come from small trials with very large effects.
- **One of those small trials was published twice.** A placebo-controlled trial from Shiraz, Iran, registered as IRCT20150202020908N2, appears in:
  - Arthritis Research & Therapy 2023 (PMID 36879307), as an intention-to-treat analysis of 44 vs 44;
  - ACR Open Rheumatology 2025 (PMID 39435687), as completers, 27 vs 27.

  The two papers share the same four core authors, the same registration and ethics codes, the same dosing and the same 4-month duration. The 2025 paper says it "was a part of a randomized double-blind placebo-controlled clinical trial" but does not say its patients are those of the 2023 paper.
- **That trial lost patients unevenly.** The 2023 paper reports that "Twenty-seven patients in the metformin group and 44 patients in the placebo group completed the study": 39% attrition on metformin, none on placebo. The 2025 paper analyses 27 placebo "counterparts" without saying how they were chosen.
- **What we could check in the meta-analyses.** We could read two of the six in full (both open access). Neither counts the Iranian trial twice:
  - Guo et al., Front Med 2025 (PMC12571718) includes only the 2023 paper.
  - Chen et al., Front Med 2026 network meta-analysis (PMC13437381) has a single Alimoradi row.

  **One more flag in the network meta-analysis.** Its Table 1 lists a third metformin trial, "Ruan 2022": 131 vs 131, sustained-release metformin titrated to 2 g/day, 24 months. Those are the planned figures of the published *protocol* for a Guangzhou trial (Ruan G ... Ding C, BMC Musculoskelet Disord 2022; PMID 35598008; NCT05034029, 262 planned).
  - We found no results publication: PubMed search for metformin + osteoarthritis by Ruan G or Ding C, 7 records, none a trial report.
  - ClinicalTrials.gov shows NCT05034029 with status unknown and no posted results.
  - The paper's reference list has no Ruan entry.

  So it is unclear what data, if any, the network meta-analysis took from "Ruan 2022". We ask, not allege.

  The other four meta-analyses (Kao 2026, Chenchula 2026, Chen Z 2026, Yao 2026) are paywalled. We have not checked them, and we do not claim they double-count.

## The randomized evidence underneath

Human randomized trials of metformin for knee OA with results, from a PubMed search (14 records, 2026-10-15; animal studies and protocols set aside):

| Trial | Registration | Design | n | Pain result as reported |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Pan F et al., JAMA 2025 (40274279), Australia | ACTRN12621000710820 | oral 2,000 mg/day vs placebo, 6 months | 54 / 53 | VAS -11.4 mm (-20.1 to -2.6); SMD 0.43 |
| Alimoradi N et al., Arthritis Res Ther 2023 (36879307), Iran | IRCT20150202020908N2 | oral 1.5 g/day vs placebo, 4 months | 44 / 44 (27 / 44 completed) | KOOS pain 54.9 → 70.8 vs 51.8 → 50.5; between-group P = 0.0001 on post-treatment scores |
| Alimoradi N et al., ACR Open Rheumatol 2025 (39435687), Iran | IRCT20150202020908N2 (same) | same trial, biomarker sub-study | 27 / 27 | KOOS pain 54.0 → 70.7 vs 52.0 → 50.5; P = 0.001 |
| Aiad AAE et al., Inflammopharmacology 2024 (38869746), Egypt | NCT05638893, registered after completion | oral 1,000 mg/day + celecoxib vs placebo + celecoxib, 12 weeks | 25 / 25 | WOMAC pain P < 0.0001 |
| Abed MS et al., J Orthop Surg Res 2024 (39456024), Egypt | PACTR202311507335269, registered retrospectively | 1.2% metformin gel by phonophoresis, no placebo-drug arm | 78 in 3 groups | all groups improved |

ClinicalTrials.gov lists 9 metformin + knee OA records. None has posted results, including the completed Egyptian trial NCT05638893.

## Why this matters

- **Prescribing and trial decisions.** Pooled SMDs of 1.3-2.0 would be very large effects for any osteoarthritis pain treatment. The only large, prospectively registered double-blind trial shows an effect of about 0.4, below its own clinically important threshold. Anyone planning a trial or an evidence package should power for about 0.4, not for the pooled values.
- **Double publication.** If a paywalled meta-analysis counted the 2023 and 2025 Iranian papers as two trials, it double-counted 27 of the same metformin patients with one of the largest effects in the literature. Readers of those four meta-analyses can check their study lists for both PMIDs.
- **Redundancy.** Six overlapping meta-analyses in nine months, most built on 2-6 small trials, is a clear case of redundant evidence synthesis.

## Is this new?

We found no published comment pointing out:
- the double publication of IRCT20150202020908N2;
- the spread across these six meta-analyses;
- the impossible negative hazard ratios in the Clin Rheumatol abstract.

## Limits

- Pain SMDs are taken from abstracts, and for Guo 2025 and Chen 2026 from the open full text. We did not recompute any pooled estimate.
- Four of the six meta-analyses were read as abstracts only.
- "Published twice" rests on identical registration and ethics codes, identical arms and dosing, and a shared author group. The 2025 paper calls itself "a part of" a trial and cites the 2023 paper, so it is not hidden duplication. The issue is that the overlap is not stated in a way a meta-analyst would catch.
- The table of meta-analyses compares estimates on different scales and populations: oral and topical, with or without diabetes, RCTs mixed with cohorts. The spread partly reflects those choices. That is part of the point.
