**Short version.** Both pre-registered predictions held. Plain BM25 over the Boolean query's words reproduces the published baseline (MAP 0.160 against 0.154; WSS@95 0.401 against 0.361; both inside the margins fixed in advance). Adding the review title and prefix matching for truncated words beats it on AP in 22 of 30 reviews (threshold 20). Two things we did not predict and report as such: the review title on its own is the best BM25 query of the five variants, and the per-review spread is wide (AP from 0.004 to 0.582, median 0.076), so the mean says little about any one review. Nothing was tuned.

## 2. Predictions as written, and what happened

**H1 (reproduction).** Rule: `bool` has mean AP within 0.04 of 0.154 and mean WSS@95 within 0.06 of 0.361.

| Measure | Wang et al. 2023, BM25 | `bool` (this run) | Difference | Margin | Held? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| MAP | 0.154 | 0.1602 | +0.0062 | 0.04 | yes |
| WSS@95 | 0.361 | 0.4009 | +0.0399 | 0.06 | yes |

**H1 held.** The other published numbers, which were not part of the rule: last_rel 6033.1 vs 5996.4; WSS@100 0.264 vs 0.2653; recall at 1/5/10/20% of the list 0.082/0.242/0.391/0.563 vs 0.1031/0.2787/0.4164/0.5642. Our harness scores a little higher on every measure. Likely reasons, none tested: today's PubMed abstracts rather than the 2018 snapshot; a different stop list and tokenizer; Wang et al. may have indexed different fields. The WSS@95 gap (0.040) is two thirds of its margin, so a stricter margin would have failed it; we say so rather than tighten anything after the fact.

**Not predicted:** the review title alone, a reference variant we included only as a floor for the Boolean query, is the best BM25 query on the abstract-level qrels on every measure except recall at 1%, and the best at content level on everything except MAP and recall at 1%. Adding the title to the Boolean words (`bool_title`) captures most but not all of that gain. The Boolean query's words, once stripped of their operators and field tags, carry many terms that are there for recall in a database search (synonyms, broad MeSH headings) and dilute a bag-of-words ranking. This is a post hoc reading; a test of it (for example, `title` plus Boolean words weighted down) would be a new pre-registration, not a change to this one.

**Spread.** Per-review AP for `bool` runs from 0.004 (CD011602, ultrasonography for alcoholic cirrhosis) to 0.582 (CD011420, urine LAM assay for tuberculosis), median 0.076 (hand count over the 30 values). Six reviews score above 0.30 and pull the mean to 0.160; for the median review a BM25 ranking puts fewer than one relevant document in ten near the top. Any claim built on this harness should be read per review, which is why the per-topic numbers are in the data file.

## 4. Exploratory: retracted records in the collection

PubMed now marks 78 topic-record pairs as retracted ("Retracted Publication" in the publication types), which are 59 distinct records (hand count: nine endometriosis-related records appear in each of CD012165, CD012179 and CD012281, and one pancreatic-surgery case report in both CD010213 and CD011515). One pair is judged relevant at the abstract level: PMID 17766684, "The expression profile of micro-RNA in endometrium and endometriosis and the influence of ovarian steroids on their expression" (2007), in CD012165 (endometrial biomarkers for endometriosis); it is not relevant at the content level there, and not relevant in the two sibling reviews. None is relevant at the content level. Twenty-two of the 78 pairs sit in the largest topic, CD009263. We found no earlier count of retractions inside the CLEF TAR collections. We do not know why any of these was retracted and make no claim about it; the list is in `tar_bench_v1_retracted.jsonl` for anyone who wants to look.

What it means for benchmarking: a screening system that ranks PMID 17766684 high in CD012165 is rewarded for finding a record a reviewer would now have to drop. At one relevant record in 30 reviews the effect on the scores here is nil; it matters more for the principle that benchmark labels age.

## 5. Limits

- Today's PubMed, not the 2018 snapshot: 1,002 records have no text, and abstracts may have changed.
- 30 diagnostic-test-accuracy reviews; nothing here transfers to intervention or qualitative reviews without a new run (CLEF 2019 has those).
- The margins for H1 were set generously because the target paper does not state its BM25 parameters or indexed fields. A reproduction within 0.006 MAP and 0.040 WSS@95 is consistent with the published number, not identical to it.
- `random` is one fixed pseudo-random order, not an average over many.
- No human has checked these numbers. The run files are in TREC format so that CLEF's own `tar_eval.py` can re-score every list.

## What changes next

The harness measures correctly against a published baseline, so it can serve as the floor for stronger rankers. Candidates, each to be pre-registered before it runs: the title-first variants above; a dense or cross-encoder re-ranker (MedCPT) against `title`; and the 2019 Intervention topics for breadth. Nothing in `tar_bench.py` is changed by this note.
