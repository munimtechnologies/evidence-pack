## What the pre-registration said

> "As far as we could read, the comparison was between whole distributions of glomeruli pooled by clinical group (Kolmogorov-Smirnov and Kruskal-Wallis tests), and the spread measure is not defined in the text we read."

That was written after reading only the first part of the paper's HTML; its Methods section 4.3.7 was cut off.

## What the GloPath paper says (read 2026-10-30, arXiv 2603.02926, HTML version)

- **Methods 4.3.7:** seven parameters were computed for each glomerulus from Bowman's capsule and tuft masks, and for each case the "median and mean values across all glomeruli in a case" were taken, giving "14 aggregated case-level features per sample". So the unit of analysis is the case, summarised by its mean and median. We found no within-case spread statistic (SD, CV or IQR).
- **Tests:** Kolmogorov-Smirnov for two groups and Kruskal-Wallis for three or more, with KS D and epsilon-squared as effect sizes.
- **Results 2.3.3 (KPMP-G):** "albuminuria was linked to increased heterogeneity in glomerular area", citing Table S30. The sentence names no feature or test; Table S30 and the albuminuria cut-offs (apparently in the supplement, Table S48) were not in the text we could read.
- **Cohort:** KPMP-G is 1,352 slides with clinical metadata and 28,242 glomeruli; the number of participants is not given in the text we read.
- **No adjustment** for mean or median glomerular size is described for the KPMP-G comparisons.

(Quotes were read through a summarising web tool and marked verbatim by it; they are short fragments, not checked against the PDF.)

## Corrected reading

GloPath's KPMP-G comparison is between groups of **case-level mean and median** glomerular areas. A KS test on those case-level values can register a difference in spread **between cases** (some people with albuminuria have much larger or much smaller glomeruli on average), which may be what "heterogeneity" refers to. It does not measure how varied the glomeruli are **within one biopsy**. So our earlier worry about pooling glomeruli across people does not describe their method; the correct point is that their analysis contains **no within-biopsy spread measure at all**.

This makes the participant-level question in our pre-registration (is the within-biopsy IQR of ln glomerular area higher at higher albuminuria?) more clearly separate from GloPath's result, not less. Our H1-H3 test something GloPath did not test, on a different segmentation (KPMP's own released vectors). If H1 passes, it is not a replication of GloPath; if H1 fails, it says nothing against GloPath. The readings fixed in the pre-registration are read with that in mind, and the results note will say so.

The NEPTUNE/CureGN study cited in the pre-registration (PMC12632648) remains the closest prior use of a per-patient spread of glomerular features (standard deviations, against remission rather than cross-sectional albuminuria, outside KPMP).
